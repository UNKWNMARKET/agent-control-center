Why it exists

Three places money leaks out of an agency

This was not built as a product first. It was built to stop three specific things that were costing one agency real commission, every month.

Leak 01

Leads that sit

You paid for it at 9:02 in the morning. Nobody called until 4:30. The other four agents on that same shared lead already did, and one of them wrote it.

Leak 02

Follow-ups nobody made

"Call me after the first." Written on a sticky note that is no longer on the monitor, by an agent who has had sixty other conversations since.

Leak 03

Paperwork collected too late

Three weeks after the sale, underwriting wants the wind mit. Nobody kept it, the customer has stopped answering, and the policy cancels. You already paid for that lead and earned that commission.

The difference

Marking a lead sold is not a dropdown

When an agent records a sale, the system asks what was actually sold — carrier, line, premium, term, policy number. Then every answer pulls in the paperwork that answer creates. Miss one and the sale does not get recorded. Not a reminder. Not a warning you can click past. The button does not work.

That is the one feature no CRM on the market has, and it is the one that pays for this software by itself. One cancelled policy a year costs more than a year of it.

How it works

What happens to a lead, start to finish

Seven steps from the vendor posting it to the renewal coming due a year later. Four of them nobody has to do.

STEP 01

It arrives

Your lead vendor posts to the private address that belongs to your agency. EverQuote, SmartFinancial, AllWebLeads, your own website form — each gets its own. No inbox, no copy and paste, no retyping a phone number at nine at night.

Automatic
STEP 02

It checks itself

The address goes against the county property roll, the vehicles against their VINs, and the phone number against do-not-call — before anybody can dial it. The lead is scored so the list is ordered by what is worth calling, not by what came in last.

Automatic
STEP 03

It lands on a desk

Assigned by round robin or by hand, with an optional first text going out on arrival. It shows up on the dashboard under "new this week" and on somebody's list of work.

Automatic
STEP 04

Your agent works it

One screen: contact, household, property, vehicles, drivers, who they are insured with now and when that runs out. Click to call through your own phone system, click to text, and set a follow-up that comes back on the day.

Your agent
STEP 05

They sell it — and the gate runs

Carrier, line, premium, term, policy number. Then every document that answer requires, attached before the sale can be recorded.

Your agent
STEP 06

The policy enters your book

Written premium, term, documents and the renewal date worked out from the effective date. Nobody types a renewal date, so nobody mistypes one.

Automatic
STEP 07

It comes back before it lapses

Forty-five days out, it puts itself on the renewal list with the premium you wrote it at. Requote it and the book compounds. Ignore it and you find out in January.

Before you sign

Things worth knowing up front

The property and permit lookup is Florida. Owner of record, just value, year built, homestead status and permit history come from Florida county data. Everything else in the system — leads, do-not-call, the sold flow, the book, renewals, reports, your team — works anywhere. If you write outside Florida, you get the whole product minus that one panel, and it is built so other states can be added.

Automatic texting is optional. If you want the system sending a first text when a lead lands, that runs through a messaging account in your own agency's name, set up with you. Click-to-text through the phone you already use needs nothing extra.

Billing is handled directly. Monthly, on a twelve-month agreement. An unpaid month suspends the logins, never the data.

Questions

What agencies ask first

Can another agency on the platform see my book?

No. Every record carries your agency on it, and the scope is applied before the query runs — not filtered out afterwards. That covers leads, customers, policies, documents, your lead source endpoints and your do-not-call list. Your suppression list never affects anyone else's calling, and theirs never affects yours.

What happens to my data if I leave?

It is yours. Ask and you get the whole thing out as spreadsheets — leads, customers, policies, activity history and the document index. There is no export fee and no waiting period.

How long does setup take?

A working day for most agencies. Your workspace, your admin account, your lead sources connected and tested with a real lead, your phone system wired up, your book imported and your staff invited. You are not handed a login and left with it.

Do my agents need training?

Very little. The top row of the app is the day's work in the order it happens — dashboard, leads, follow-ups, renewals, customers, book. Everything else is filed under Tools. The one thing worth sitting with an agent on is the sold flow, because it is the one part that behaves differently from what they are used to.

Can I use my existing phone system?

Yes. Pick it once in settings: RingCentral on web, desktop or mobile, Dialpad, Zoom Phone, 8x8, Vonage, GoTo Connect, or whatever this computer already uses for calls and texts. Clicking a number then dials through it.

What if my agent just lies to get past the document gate?

They can answer "no" to a question, and that answer is recorded against the policy with their name on it — so the file shows why a document was never asked for. The gate removes the honest mistake, which is the one that actually costs you policies. The dishonest one leaves a trail.

Why a twelve-month agreement?

Because setup is included and done properly, and because a CRM only pays for itself once a book is in it and renewals start coming back round. Twelve months is one full renewal cycle.

Who actually built this?

A working insurance agent, inside a working agency, to solve problems that were costing that agency money. It is in daily use. You are not the pilot customer for someone's idea of what agencies need.
